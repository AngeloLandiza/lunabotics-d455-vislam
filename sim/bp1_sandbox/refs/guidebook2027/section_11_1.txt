11.1 Arena Composition
1. The arena contains Black Point-1 (BP-1), a crushed basalt lunar regolith simulant to a depth of
   approximately 45 cm. Coefficient of friction and cohesion are highly dependent on the humidity, bulk
   density and porosity. For more information, refer to https://ares.jsc.nasa.gov/projects/simulants/bp-
   1.html). Note the following:
       a. It does not behave like sand.
       b. The coefficient of friction has not been measured.
       c. There are naturally occurring rocks in the aggregate.
       d. BP-1 is made from crushed basalt fines and not commercially available.
       e. Reference Soil Test Apparatus for Lunar Surfaces.
       f.   The density of the compacted BP-1 aggregate will be between 1.5 g/cm3 and 1.8 g/cm3.
       g. BP-1 behaves like a silty powder soil with most particles under 100 microns in diameter.
       h. Will be compacted and the top layer will be raked to a fluffy condition of approximately 0.75
          g/cm3, similar to the Lunar surface.
       i.   Teams are encouraged to develop or procure simulants based on basaltic minerals and
            lunar surface regolith particle size, shape, and distribution.
2. There is naturally occurring additional material randomly distributed that may be mixed with the BP-
   1. This gravel material may be approximately 2 cm diameter.
3. BP-1 Reflectivity: NASA performed tests to answer questions about BP-1 reflectivity for LIDAR (or
   other LASER-based) navigation systems. The laser is not a beam – it is spread out as a sheet that
   is oriented in the vertical direction, so it is draped across the BP-1 and across a white/gray/black
   target that is standing up behind the BP-1 in the images. The BP-1 is the mound at the bottom of
   each image. Teams can get the reflectivity of the BP-1 by comparing the brightness of the laser
   sheet seen reflected from the BP-1 with the brightness of the same sheet reflected from the white
   and black portions of the target.
       a. Note the BP-1 is mounded. Teams need to account for the fact that it is not a flat surface for
          analyzing the brightness in the images. The three pictures below were shot with the camera
          at 10, 16, and 21 degrees relative to the surface.
